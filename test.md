

Below is the end-to-end service workflow of a residential HVAC company, explained as a
real business process. This applies to companies that install, repair, replace, and maintain air
conditioners, furnaces, heat pumps, ductwork, thermostats, and indoor air quality systems for
homeowners.

- Lead Generation and Customer

continuous flow of leads.

- Customer Inquiry and Initial Contact
Once a customer contacts the company, the office team or call center handles the inquiry.
The first interaction is very important because residential HVAC customers often contact
multiple companies. Fast response increases conversion.
The customer may say things like:
“My AC is running but not cooling.”
“My furnace stopped working.”
“I need a quote to replace my HVAC system.”
“I want seasonal maintenance.”
The customer service representative usually asks diagnostic questions:

## 9. Technician Job Preparation
Before arriving, the technician reviews the job details.

They may check:
● Customer information
● Issue description
● Equipment history
● Previous invoices
● Previous technician notes
● Maintenance membership status
● Warranty status
● Photos or attachments
● Recommended parts
● Safety notes

Customer had capacitor replaced last year. AC is now not cooling. System is 14

- Technician Arrival and Customer
## Greeting
When the technician arrives, they usually:
- Mark job as “Arrived”
- Greet the customer
- Confirm the reported issue
- Ask follow-up questions
- Explain the diagnostic process
- Get authorization for diagnostic fee if applicable
## Example:
“I understand your AC is running but not cooling. I’ll inspect the thermostat, indoor
unit, outdoor unit, refrigerant levels, electrical components, and airflow. The
diagnostic fee is $89, and I’ll explain the repair options before doing any work.”
This step is important for trust and transparency.

## Final Simple Definition
A residential HVAC company service workflow is the complete process of:
Attracting a homeowner, understanding their heating/cooling problem, scheduling
the right technician, diagnosing the system, giving an estimate, completing the
repair or installation, collecting payment, following up with the customer, and using
that relationship for future maintenance, replacement, and retention opportunities.
